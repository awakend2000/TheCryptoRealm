title: "Bitcoin Slides to $76,000 as the AI Trade Cracks"
description: "Wall Street's worst day since April 2025 dragged bitcoin and crypto lower. Here's what happened, why analysts say it happened, and what it shows about leverage."
date: 2026-11-06
Stocks had a brutal Thursday, and crypto went down with them. Bitcoin slipped about 5% to just under $76,000 as a sell-off in AI-linked stocks swept through the markets, wiping out the bounce that followed the U.S. midterm elections.
What happened
On Thursday, Nov. 5, U.S. stocks had their worst session since April 2025:
The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5%, the S&P 500 about 4.3%, and the Dow about 3.4%.
The Nasdaq 100, which leans more heavily on big tech, dropped about 6.4%, its worst day since March 2020.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell around 12%.
Crypto took a hit too, though a smaller one than many AI stocks. Ether fell about 7.5% to roughly $2,240, and XRP and Solana each lost around 5.5%.
Leverage did the damage
In the 24 hours to Thursday's close, more than $1 billion in leveraged crypto bets were wiped out, according to CoinGlass data reported by CoinDesk. Most of them were bets on prices going up.
Here is how that works. A trader borrows money to make a bigger bet. If the price falls far enough, the exchange automatically sells the position. That forced selling pushes prices down further, which can trigger more forced selling. It was the biggest wipeout since Oct. 28–29, when bitcoin fell from nearly $82,000 to $76,000.
Crypto-linked stocks fared worse than the coins themselves. Bitcoin miners dropped 15% to 20%, and digital-asset-related stocks fell more than 10%, CoinDesk reported.
Why analysts say it happened
Analysts at crypto trading firms FalconX and GSR pointed to a few things:
Worries about AI data centers. CoinDesk reported that Tuesday's midterm results gave Democrats control of both chambers of Congress. The analysts said investors fear stronger political opposition could slow the building of AI data centers. That matters to bitcoin miners, because AI computing has become a bigger part of their business.
Doubts about AI spending. GSR said investors were already asking whether the huge sums going into AI infrastructure would pay off, and disappointing chipmaker earnings added to the pressure.
A short-lived rally. FalconX said Wednesday's post-election bounce was driven largely by traders closing out bets against the market, so it had little staying power.
The Fed. Markets.com also noted that Fed officials were backing gradual tightening, which adds pressure on risk assets.
Gold vs. bitcoin
While stocks and crypto fell together, gold rose about 0.6% to roughly $4,094 an ounce. Bitcoin, on the other hand, moved with the stock market.
Jasper De Maere of trading firm Wintermute cautioned against reading too much into one day. He said crypto held up relatively well and that the medium-term outlook was still "glass half full."
Our take (opinion)
Two things stand out to us.
First, a day like this shows that bitcoin still trades like a risk asset in the short term. "Digital gold" is a long-term idea, not a daily hedge. But the reasons many of us care about crypto don't change with the price on any given Thursday: owning your own money, moving it without a middleman, and not needing anyone's permission.
Second, borrowed money is what turns a bad day into a disaster. Price swings are part of crypto. Forced liquidations are a choice. If you're new, learn how leverage works before you go near it.
